title: "What Does the Government Buy? More Than Most Small Businesses Realize"
---

<p>One of the most common questions small business owners ask is:</p><p><strong>“Would the government actually buy what my company sells?”</strong></p><p>In many cases, the answer is yes.</p><p>Government agencies operate offices, hospitals, military installations, schools, airports, parks, courthouses, laboratories, transportation systems, and public facilities. To keep those operations running, they purchase an enormous range of commercial products and professional services.</p><p>The federal government uses Product and Service Codes to identify products, services, and research and development purchased through federal contract actions. GSA purchasing programs alone give eligible government buyers access to millions of commercial products and services.</p>

<p>Government agencies regularly purchase professional support services, including:</p>
<ul data-spread="false">
<li>Accounting and bookkeeping</li>
<li>Human resources consulting</li>
<li>Temporary staffing</li>
<li>Legal support</li>
<li>Marketing and communications</li>
<li>Management consulting</li>
<li>Translation and interpretation</li>
<li>Records management</li>
<li>Training and curriculum development</li>
<li>Program and project management</li>
<li>Call center services</li>
<li>Data entry and administrative support</li>
</ul>
<p>A service does not need to be unusual or defense-related to have government demand. Agencies need many of the same business services purchased by private-sector organizations.</p>

<p>Construction represents a major portion of public-sector purchasing at every level of government.</p>
<p>Opportunities may include:</p>
<ul data-spread="false">

<li>Environmental remediation</li>

<p>Small businesses can participate as prime contractors, subcontractors, suppliers, or specialty trade contractors.</p>
<p>State and local transportation projects can also create significant opportunities for certified Disadvantaged Business Enterprises and other small or diverse contractors.</p>

<p>Businesses interested in emergency work should complete the proper registrations before a disaster occurs. Waiting until an emergency has already been declared can cause the company to miss early purchasing activity.</p>

<p>Do not rely on assumptions. Research actual government purchasing behavior.</p>
<p>Start by identifying your company’s:</p>
<ol start="1" data-spread="false">
<li><strong>NAICS codes:</strong> These classify the industries in which your company operates.</li>
<li><strong>Product and Service Codes:</strong> These describe what the federal government purchased.</li>
<li><strong>Target agencies:</strong> Focus on organizations whose missions create demand for your work.</li>
<li><strong>Service area:</strong> Determine where your company can realistically perform.</li>
<li><strong>Past awards:</strong> Review which agencies purchased similar products, from whom, and at what value.</li>
<li><strong>Current opportunities:</strong> Search SAM.gov and relevant state or local procurement systems.</li>
</ol>
<p>SAM.gov allows businesses to search federal contract opportunities, follow notices, save searches, and review award data.</p>

<p>Not every federal contractor needs a GSA Multiple Award Schedule.</p>
<p>A GSA Schedule is one purchasing vehicle used by government buyers, but many contracts are competed through SAM.gov, agency-specific systems, simplified purchasing procedures, subcontracting relationships, and other contract vehicles.</p>
<p>Before pursuing a GSA Schedule, determine whether your target agencies actually use that vehicle to purchase what your company sells.</p>

<p><strong>Do I need a SAM registration for state or local contracts?</strong></p><p>Usually not. SAM.gov is primarily used for federal awards. State and local agencies generally maintain their own vendor registration and procurement systems.</p>

<p><strong>Can my business pursue SLED and federal contracts at the same time?</strong></p><p>Yes. Many companies pursue local, state, and federal opportunities simultaneously, provided they have the resources to manage the registrations, bids, and resulting contracts.</p>

<p><strong>Is a government certification required to win contracts?</strong></p><p>Not always. Businesses can win many openly competed contracts without a socioeconomic certification. However, the right certification may create access to restricted opportunities, subcontracting programs, purchasing preferences, and buyer outreach.</p>
