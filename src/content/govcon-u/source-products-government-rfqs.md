---
title: "How to Source Products for Government RFQs"
---

<p>Federal agencies publish thousands of requests for quotations involving commercial products, equipment, replacement parts, supplies, and materials.</p><p>Many of these opportunities identify:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>A manufacturer</li><li>A brand name</li><li>A model number</li><li>A national stock number</li><li>A manufacturer part number</li><li>A detailed product description</li><li>Required salient characteristics</li></ul><p>You do not necessarily need to manufacture the product to submit a quotation. Small distributors and resellers can compete—but only when they understand how to source, document, price, and deliver the requested item correctly.</p>

<p>Begin with the solicitation—not Google.</p><p>Identify whether the RFQ requires:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>The exact identified brand and part number</li><li>A brand-name-or-equal product</li><li>An approved source</li><li>A new product</li><li>Original equipment manufacturer parts</li><li>Specific packaging</li><li>A specific country of origin</li><li>An authorized distributor</li><li>A required delivery date</li></ul><p>When an RFQ states <strong>brand name or equal</strong>, an alternative product must satisfy the stated physical, functional, and performance characteristics. The bidder must clearly identify the proposed brand and model and provide enough information for the government to evaluate it.</p><p>Do not quote an “equivalent” product simply because it looks similar or costs less.</p>

<p>When an RFQ includes a manufacturer part number, search the exact number using quotation marks.</p><p>Then identify:</p><ol start="1" data-spread="false"><li>The original manufacturer</li><li>The manufacturer’s authorized distributors</li><li>Industrial or government-focused wholesalers</li><li>Regional distributors</li><li>Master distributors that sell only through resellers</li><li>Suppliers with confirmed inventory</li></ol><p>Do not rely solely on retail marketplace listings.</p><p>A low online price may involve:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>Used or refurbished products</li><li>Gray-market inventory</li><li>Counterfeit items</li><li>Incorrect packaging</li><li>Discontinued models</li><li>No manufacturer warranty</li><li>Unverified country of origin</li><li>Inventory that is not actually available</li></ul><p>Whenever possible, obtain a written quote directly from the manufacturer or an authorized distribution channel.</p>

<p>Your supplier request should include:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>Manufacturer name</li><li>Exact part number</li><li>Required quantity</li><li>Delivery destination</li><li>Required delivery date</li><li>Packaging requirements</li><li>Quote-validity period</li><li>Government end-user information</li><li>Applicable certification status</li><li>Resale-certificate information</li></ul><p>Ask the supplier to confirm:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>Unit price</li><li>Available quantity</li><li>Lead time</li><li>Freight</li><li>Warranty</li><li>Country of manufacture</li><li>Whether the item is new and unused</li><li>Whether it is an exact match</li><li>Whether pricing will remain valid through award</li><li>Whether the supplier will reserve inventory</li></ul><p>A supplier quote is not useful when it expires before the government expects to make an award.</p>

<p>USAspending.gov can help identify businesses receiving federal awards within a particular industry.</p><p>Search using:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>Manufacturing NAICS codes</li><li>Product and Service Codes</li><li>Agency names</li><li>Award descriptions</li><li>Manufacturer names</li><li>Known incumbent contractors</li><li>Product-related keywords</li></ul><p>USAspending is the federal government’s official open-data source for federal award information, and its advanced search allows users to research awards by recipient, industry, agency, location, and other criteria.</p><p>This research can reveal:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>Manufacturers already selling to federal agencies</li><li>Distributors receiving similar awards</li><li>Agencies buying the product</li><li>Historical award values</li><li>Likely competitors</li><li>Recurring product demand</li></ul><p>You can then contact manufacturers and explain that your business is interested in serving as a government reseller or certified contracting partner.</p>

<p>Do not simply email:</p><blockquote><p>“Can I get your best price?”</p></blockquote><p>Explain the opportunity.</p><p>Tell the manufacturer:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>Which agency is purchasing</li><li>The estimated quantity</li><li>The required delivery location</li><li>The quotation deadline</li><li>Whether the procurement is set aside</li><li>Whether your company holds relevant certifications</li><li>Whether you need authorization documentation</li><li>Whether the manufacturer would be named in your quotation</li></ul><p>Some manufacturers do not actively pursue smaller government RFQs. Others prefer to sell through distributors rather than establish direct government registrations, prepare quotations, manage compliance, and process individual orders.</p><p>A capable small or certified reseller can help the manufacturer access this business without requiring it to compete directly.</p><p>Your value is not merely forwarding an order. It is managing the government-sales process.</p>

<p>On certain small-business supply set-asides, a reseller may need to comply with the SBA’s nonmanufacturer rule.</p><p>This rule allows a qualifying small business to supply a product it did not manufacture. However, the product generally must be manufactured by another small business unless SBA has issued an applicable class waiver or the contracting officer obtains an individual waiver.</p><p>Do not assume that you can offer any brand simply because you are a small business.</p><p>Review:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>The solicitation’s assigned NAICS code</li><li>FAR 52.219-33</li><li>Any nonmanufacturer-rule language</li><li>Applicable SBA waivers</li><li>Manufacturer size</li><li>Place of manufacture</li></ul><p>When uncertain, submit a written question before the RFQ deadline.</p>

<p>Federal supply acquisitions may contain Buy American, Trade Agreements, Department of Defense, or other country-of-origin requirements.</p><p>For example, acquisitions containing FAR 52.225-5 generally require delivery of U.S.-made or designated-country end products.</p><p>Never guess where a product was manufactured.</p><p>Request written confirmation from the manufacturer or distributor and retain it with your quotation records.</p><p>The words “ships from the United States” do not mean “manufactured in the United States.”</p>

<p>Your bid price should account for more than the supplier’s unit cost.</p><p>Consider:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>Product cost</li><li>Inbound and outbound freight</li><li>Fuel or residential-delivery surcharges</li><li>Packaging</li><li>Insurance</li><li>Payment-processing fees</li><li>Financing or credit costs</li><li>Inspection requirements</li><li>Warranty administration</li><li>Returns or replacements</li><li>Labor</li><li>Overhead</li><li>Profit</li><li>Risk of supplier price changes</li></ul><p>Also confirm whether the government is requesting:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>FOB destination pricing</li><li>FOB origin pricing</li><li>Separate freight</li><li>An all-inclusive unit price</li><li>A total lot price</li></ul><p>A low quote that loses money is not a successful government contract.</p>

<p>Before submitting your quotation, reconfirm:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>Inventory</li><li>Lead time</li><li>Price validity</li><li>Freight</li><li>Exact model</li><li>Country of origin</li><li>Manufacturer size, when relevant</li><li>Authorization status</li><li>Delivery requirements</li></ul><p>Government awards are not always made immediately after quotations are submitted.</p><p>If inventory is limited, ask whether the supplier can hold it. When that is not possible, include sufficient pricing protection and have a secondary source available.</p>

<p><strong>Can I bid on a product RFQ without owning inventory?</strong></p><p>Yes, provided you have a reliable source and can meet the solicitation’s delivery and compliance requirements. Do not bid based on unconfirmed online availability.</p>

<p><strong>Can I offer an alternative brand?</strong></p><p>Only when the solicitation permits alternatives, such as through brand-name-or-equal language. The proposed item must meet every listed salient characteristic.</p>

<p><strong>Do manufacturers work with certified government resellers?</strong></p><p>Many manufacturers use distributors and reseller networks. A certification may make your business more valuable when the opportunity is restricted or when a manufacturer wants greater participation in small-business and supplier-diversity markets.</p>

<p><strong>Where can I find government product RFQs?</strong></p><p>Federal contract opportunities can be searched through SAM.gov. Anyone can search opportunities without an account, while an account allows users to save searches and follow notices.</p>

<p>Successful product contractors build relationships before they need them.</p><p>Maintain a sourcing database containing:</p><ul data-spread="false"><li>Manufacturer contacts</li><li>Authorized distributors</li><li>Product categories</li><li>NAICS and PSC codes</li><li>Quote terms</li><li>Lead times</li><li>Country-of-origin information</li><li>Small-business status</li><li>Previous RFQs</li><li>Award results</li></ul><p>Over time, your supplier network becomes a competitive advantage.</p><p>govCERTS helps small businesses establish government registrations, obtain relevant certifications, improve their federal profiles, and prepare compliant bids for product and service opportunities.</p><p><strong>Find the product. Verify compliance. Price the risk. Deliver exactly what you promised.</strong></p>
