---
title: "How to Position Your Company for Government Contracting Success"
---

<p>Registering your company does not automatically make it competitive.</p><p>To succeed in government contracting, your business must be easy to find, easy to understand, and present when buyers are researching the market.</p>

<p>Start with focused and accurate NAICS and Product and Service Codes.</p>
<p>Do not select every code that could loosely relate to your business. Your codes should support services or products you can credibly deliver, price, and explain.</p>
<p>Next, create a professional capabilities statement that presents your:</p>
<ul data-spread="false">
<li>Core competencies</li>
<li>Differentiators</li>
<li>Past performance</li>
<li>Certifications</li>
<li>Government identifiers</li>
<li>Contact information</li>
</ul>
<p>Think of it as your government-contracting résumé—not a general marketing brochure.</p>
<p>Your SBA Small Business Search profile should reinforce the same message. Contracting officers are required to use SBS as part of small-business market research, making a complete, keyword-rich profile an important visibility tool.</p>

<p>Once the foundation is complete, stop relying exclusively on bid websites.</p>
<p>Attend:</p>
<ul data-spread="false">
<li>Agency industry days</li>
<li>Contracting-officer meet-and-greets</li>
<li>Small-business matchmaking events</li>
<li>Prime-contractor outreach events</li>
<li>Pre-proposal conferences</li>
<li>Site visits and walkthroughs</li>
<li>Relevant trade and industry events</li>
</ul>
<p>Respond to sources-sought notices. Introduce your company to agency small-business specialists. Ask informed questions. Follow up professionally.</p>
<p>These activities help buyers understand that your business exists, is interested, and may be capable of supporting an upcoming requirement.</p>

<p>Government buyers cannot include your business in their research if they cannot find it. They cannot develop confidence in your capabilities if they never hear from you.</p>
<p>Strong positioning comes from combining:</p>
<ul data-spread="false">
<li>Accurate registrations</li>
<li>Relevant certifications</li>
<li>A clear capabilities statement</li>
<li>An optimized SBS profile</li>
<li>Market research</li>
<li>Consistent buyer outreach</li>
<li>In-person and virtual participation</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not wait until a solicitation is released to introduce your company.</p>
<p><strong>Build your foundation. Show up early. Be seen. Be present.</strong></p>
